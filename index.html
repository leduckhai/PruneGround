01 · Motivation

Ground locally,
not globally.

Most 3D grounding systems reason over every proposal in a scene. But referring expressions usually depend on a small neighborhood: the target, one or two anchors, and their spatial relations.

Full-scene reasoning
?

Clutter creates ambiguity

Same-class distractors and imperfect segmentation enlarge the candidate set, increase computation, and propagate errors.

→
PruneGround

Language narrows the search

A frozen VLM keeps only the relevant spatial region, then adds visual context to make the query easier to ground.

03 · Method

Three stages.
One focused search.

The framework progressively reduces spatial noise and improves language structure before predicting the target 3D bounding box.

01Pruning

Language-Guided
Spatial Pruning

LGSP renders one top view plus four oblique RGB-depth views. Conditioned on the query, a frozen VLM predicts a relevant 2D region that is projected back into the 3D point cloud.

  • Removes language-irrelevant points
  • Reduces same-class distractors
  • Retains full height along the z-axis
02Multi-view

Multi-View-Conditioned Description Reformulation

MCDR uses local side views to split free-form language into compact target–anchor statements, then augments missing spatial cues visible in the scene.

  • Simplifies complex expressions
  • Adds visually grounded anchors
  • Encodes explicit spatial relations
03Spatial LLM

LLM-based
Grounder

A detection-pretrained SpatialLM is repurposed for language-conditioned grounding. Point features and text tokens are jointly decoded into one target box.

  • Sonata point-cloud encoder
  • Qwen2.5-0.5B language decoder
  • Single target-selective prediction

05 · Limitation

Early errors
can travel downstream.

A three-stage pipeline can propagate errors.

Low-quality renderings, occlusion, incomplete point clouds, or mistakes in LGSP and MCDR can remove the target or distort its relations. Those early errors may then be amplified by the final grounder.
